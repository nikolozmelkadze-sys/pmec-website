How to Qualify for PMP Certification in Georgia

By Nikoloz Melkadze, PMI-CI · 8 min read · Updated May 2025

The PMP (Project Management Professional) is the most recognised project management credential worldwide — accepted by international organisations, Georgian banks, telecoms, NGOs, and government contractors alike. But before you can sit the exam, you need to meet PMI's eligibility requirements.

This guide walks you through exactly what PMI requires, how to count your project experience, what commonly disqualifies candidates, and what to do if you are not quite there yet. If you have ever wondered "do I qualify for PMP?" — this article gives you a definitive answer.

Quick summary

PMI has two eligibility tracks: one for degree holders, one for everyone else. Both require documented project leadership experience and 35 hours of formal PM education. If you have been managing projects in any capacity for 3+ years, there is a good chance you already qualify.

The two eligibility tracks

PMI defines eligibility based on your highest level of formal education. There are two distinct paths:

Your education level Project experience required PM education required
4-year degree (Bachelor's or higher) 36 months leading projects 35 contact hours
High school diploma or secondary education 60 months leading projects 35 contact hours

The 35 contact hours of PM education is a hard requirement for both tracks — you cannot sit the exam without it. This is exactly what PMEC's 35-Hour PMP Training Programme is designed to satisfy.

Important

The experience must be from within the last 8 years. Experience older than 8 years does not count toward the requirement.

What counts as "leading projects"?

This is where most Georgian professionals either undercount or overclaim their experience. PMI's definition is specific: you must have led and directed projects — not simply participated in them as a team member.

Leading a project means you were responsible for:

  • Defining project scope, objectives, or deliverables
  • Planning timelines, budgets, or resource allocation
  • Coordinating a team or stakeholders toward a goal
  • Managing risks, issues, or changes on the project
  • Being accountable for the project's outcome

Your job title does not need to include "Project Manager." Many eligible candidates in Georgia hold titles such as Department Head, Programme Coordinator, Team Lead, Operations Manager, or Deputy Director. What matters is the nature of the work, not what your business card says.

Georgian context

Professionals working in international organisations, NGOs, defence, banking, telecoms, or public administration in Georgia frequently have qualifying project leadership experience — even if their organisation does not use formal PM methodology. Coordinating a government programme, leading an EU-funded initiative, or managing a team through a system implementation all count.

How to count your project months

PMI counts experience in non-overlapping months of project leadership, not total years of employment. This distinction matters and frequently surprises candidates.

Non-overlapping means

If you led three simultaneous projects for 12 months, that counts as 12 months of experience — not 36. You cannot count the same calendar month multiple times, even if you were leading several projects at once.

How to calculate your months

  1. List every project where you led or directed the work
  2. For each project, record the start and end month
  3. Map all projects onto a single calendar timeline
  4. Count the number of unique calendar months where you were actively leading at least one project
  5. Exclude months where your role was purely execution (team member only, no leadership responsibility)
Example calculation

You led Project A from Jan 2021 – Dec 2021 (12 months), Project B from Jun 2021 – Jun 2022 (overlaps with A for 7 months), and Project C from Jan 2023 – Dec 2023 (12 months). Your non-overlapping total: 12 + 5 (non-overlapping B months) + 12 = 29 months. A degree holder needs 36, so you would need 7 more qualifying months.

Real scenarios from Georgian professionals

Scenario 1

Operations Manager at a Georgian bank, 6 years in role

Manages cross-department process improvement initiatives, led the rollout of a new core banking system across 3 branches, coordinates vendors and internal teams. Holds a Master's degree.

Very likely eligible. System rollouts and cross-department initiatives typically qualify as projects. 6 years gives ample non-overlapping months. The Master's degree means only 36 months required.

Scenario 2

Programme Coordinator at an international NGO, 4 years

Coordinates EU-funded grants, manages deliverable timelines and donor reporting, leads a small team of 4. Bachelor's degree.

Eligible. Grant management with team leadership and stakeholder reporting is exactly the kind of project leadership PMI requires. 4 years with a degree comfortably exceeds the 36-month threshold.

Scenario 3

IT specialist at a telecom company, 5 years

Works on infrastructure projects but primarily as an executor — implementing configurations set by others, attending project meetings, delivering assigned tasks. No formal project leadership responsibility. Bachelor's degree.

Likely not eligible yet. Executing tasks within a project does not count as leading it. However, if this person can document specific periods where they led a sub-project or coordinated a team, those months could count. A free eligibility check would clarify.

Scenario 4

Department Head at a government ministry, 3 years post-secondary (no degree)

Leads departmental reform initiatives, coordinates with other ministries, manages a team of 12 and an annual budget. No university degree — professional secondary education only.

Borderline. Without a degree, 60 months are required. 3 years = 36 months, so this person needs roughly 2 more years of qualifying experience. Strong candidate to start the programme now and apply to PMI once the months accumulate.

The 35 contact hours requirement — what it means

The third requirement is straightforward but strictly enforced: you must complete 35 hours of formal project management education from a recognised provider before PMI will accept your application.

This cannot be self-study, reading PM books, or on-the-job learning. It must be a structured programme from an accredited provider — classroom, online, or blended. PMI issues a certificate of completion, which you attach to your application.

What happens during PMI's audit process

Roughly 25% of PMP applicants are selected for audit by PMI after submitting their application. Being audited is not a red flag — it is a random process. It does mean you need to have your documentation ready.

If audited, PMI will ask for:

  • Copies of your educational certificates
  • Signatures from supervisors or managers confirming your project experience claims
  • Your 35-hour education certificate

This is why honest, accurate documentation of your project experience is essential. Do not round up months or claim leadership on projects where you were a team member. PMI has revoked certifications and banned candidates for falsified applications.

Frequently asked questions about PMP eligibility in Georgia

Does experience in Georgia's public sector count?

Yes. Project leadership in any sector — public, private, NGO, international organisation, or military — counts toward the PMI experience requirement. What matters is the nature of the work: did you lead and direct the project?

My employer does not use formal project management methodology. Does that disqualify me?

No. PMI does not require you to have used a specific methodology (Waterfall, Agile, PRINCE2, etc.) during your project experience. Informal project leadership still counts. Many Georgian professionals lead projects without ever calling them "projects" — the substance of the work is what PMI evaluates.

I have international work experience. Does it count?

Yes. PMI accepts project experience from any country. Work completed during international assignments, study abroad, or employment with foreign organisations is fully eligible.

Can I use volunteer or non-paid project experience?

Yes, with some conditions. PMI accepts non-paid project leadership experience as long as it is legitimate, verifiable, and you held genuine leadership responsibility. You will still need someone to sign off on it during a potential audit.
